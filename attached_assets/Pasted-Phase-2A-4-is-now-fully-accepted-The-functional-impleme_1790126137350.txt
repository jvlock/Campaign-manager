5. Evaluation classifications

Assign exactly one primary classification to every missing rule:

A. Deterministic with existing context

The current typed context already contains everything required to decide the rule.

B. Deterministic using verified planning output

The rule should consume the scheduling, audience-state, business-day, or readiness result rather than reimplementing that logic.

C. Deterministic after typed-context expansion

The rule can be automated, but an explicit typed field or evidence object must be added first.

D. Manual evidence required

A person must attest to, review, approve, or upload evidence. Absence of evidence must produce unavailable, incomplete, or failed status according to the canonical rule—not an automatic pass.

E. Governed-service dependency

The rule requires the unresolved governed naming, taxonomy, campaign-code, or UTM service.

These rules remain non-bypassable. A missing service cannot be represented as a pass or satisfied through an unauthorized dependency exception.

F. External-system dependency

The rule depends on event-platform, landing-page, consent, attendance, asset, or other external evidence not available in the current in-memory layer.

G. Structural or containment guarantee

The requirement is satisfied by type design or absence of prohibited capability rather than a session-data predicate. Explain whether it should remain a structural test or also receive a reporting adapter. Do not invent a meaningless evaluator merely to increase the coverage number.

H. Genuine business ambiguity

The canonical rule cannot be implemented consistently without a product-owner decision. State the exact ambiguity and viable choices.

Do not classify implementation inconvenience as business ambiguity.
